## Accretion flows around black holes

Broadly, my work at ICTS is on understanding accretion flows around black holes through numerical experiments and modelling.

The infall of matter towards these compact objects is complex and highly non-linear. These flows host some of the most extreme and energetic events in the universe, and the infall itself is one of them.

More often than not, the matter falling in is at very high temperatures. As it moves faster and faster, its atoms are ionised and it becomes a plasma of charged particles. A huge number of these particles are in motion, and together they can host magnetic fields. The flow then has particles interacting with each other while also interacting with the magnetic field, and vice versa. We solve the equations that govern these flows, the magnetic fields and their coupling, using general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations.

Then there are the interesting phenomena that happen because of the background spacetime curvature, jets being one example. We now have sophisticated telescopes that capture these phenomena directly and indirectly, and we observe them from afar.

## What I do

I study some of these phenomena by solving the equations, which needs heavy computing. Sometimes I compare the results with what we observe, using ray tracing to turn simulations into synthetic images and spectra.

## Featured project: How magnetic fields grow and sustain

For example, one of my projects studied how these magnetic fields "grow" and "sustain" in the flow (the dynamo process), and how this specific process leaves its impact on the flow and the jets.
